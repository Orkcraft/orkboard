# Before the retro

Write one page the person reads before the retro. It is about **the sprint and the work**, never about a person.

## Read

1. Find the Jira site (its cloud id), the project or board the person named, and the sprint (its name or dates). Not
   found, or more than one: say so in the doubts.
2. The sprint's issues: taken at its start and added later, done, carried over, reopened.
3. The pull requests of the repositories named over the sprint: how long each waited for its first review.
4. Slack: list the channels, then read only the channels the person named, over the sprint. Never a direct message,
   never a channel they did not name.
5. *Last time* (when given): the last retro's brief. No last retro: say so in the doubts.

## Write the brief (Markdown)

- **The sprint in facts** — taken vs done, carried over, reopened, the median wait for a review; the issues with links.
- **Last retro's actions** — each action from *Last time*, and what came of it, with links where there are any.
- **What the channels said went wrong** — as items, with links, no names.
- **Topics** — at most 5, as questions to the team.

## The line

- Facts with links; questions, not conclusions. A person is named only as the owner or the author of an item.
- No strengths or weaknesses, no score, no ranking, no comparison of people, no reading of anyone's tone or mood, no
  counts of commits, messages or hours as a measure of anyone.
- Invent no fact, no name, no date, no number: what you could not find goes into the doubts.
- Write in the language the person wrote in.
